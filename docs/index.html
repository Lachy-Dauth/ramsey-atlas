What does R mean?

R(k₁, …, kᵣ) is the smallest number of vertices that forces a clique of size kᵢ in colour i for some i, in every r-colouring of the edges of a complete graph. The order of the arguments does not matter.

For equal arguments, Rᵣ(k) = R(k, …, k). This is a diagonal Ramsey number.

Two endpoints, one unknown

Lower bound L: there is a colouring on L − 1 vertices avoiding all the required cliques. Upper bound U: every colouring on U vertices has one.

When L = U, the number is known exactly. An interval records the reported mathematical bounds, not an estimate or a conjecture.

A dated research snapshot

This is a curated catalogue of small classical clique Ramsey numbers, not an exhaustive database of all Ramsey variants. Each entry names its sources. Reported computational bounds and preprints are labelled.

New results may appear after the last literature check. The catalogue covers 58 cases: two-colour arguments up to 10, triangle cases through 15, and selected cases in three to nine colours.

Built on the literature

The main reference is Stanisław P. Radziszowski’s Small Ramsey Numbers, a regularly updated survey. Later results are identified in the individual entries.
